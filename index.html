Essential Exam Advice

Common Student Misconceptions & Pitfalls

Avoid losing marks on A-Level exams by recognizing these frequent mistakes.

1. Forgetting Distance Unit Conversions

Distance in Coulomb's Law must always be in meters (m). Exam questions often give separation in centimeters (cm) or millimeters (mm).
Fix: Convert cm ⇒ × 10⁻² m and mm ⇒ × 10⁻³ m before squaring!

2. Omitting the Square on Distance (r²)

A common error is to omit the square on $r$ when evaluating the expression, using $\frac{k q_1 q_2}{r}$ instead of $\frac{k q_1 q_2}{r^2}$.
Fix: Double check your calculator parenthesis: /(r^2).

3. Misinterpreting Negative Sign in Vectors

Plugging negative charge signs into the force formula can lead to incorrect vector directions in 2D problems.
Fix: Use the absolute value $|q_1 q_2|$ to calculate the force magnitude, then indicate the force direction on a vector diagram.

4. Confusing Electric Force (F) and Electric Field (E)

Force $F$ requires two charges ($F = \frac{k q_1 q_2}{r^2}$ in Newtons), whereas Electric Field Strength $E$ requires one source charge ($E = \frac{k Q}{r^2}$ in N C⁻¹).

5. Measuring r to the Wrong Point

Coulomb’s law uses the separation between the centres of the charges. Using the gap between surfaces gives a force that is too large.
Fix: Add the radii of spheres to any surface-to-surface gap first.

6. Mixing Up Direction and Sign

The sign of a charge tells you attract or repel. It does not tell you left or right.
Fix: Decide attract or repel first, then draw each force along the line joining the charges.

7. Leaving Charges in µC or nC

Putting 3.0 for a 3.0 µC charge makes the force 10¹² times too large.
Fix: Write every charge in coulombs (µC × 10⁻⁶, nC × 10⁻⁹) before substituting.
